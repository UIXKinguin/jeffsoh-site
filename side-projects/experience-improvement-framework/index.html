Experience Improvement Framework

A practical framework for spotting and fixing dark patterns in mobile games.

Field
Human-Computer Interaction
Role
Head Researcher

Problem

Existing dark-pattern taxonomies were fragmented and too academic to guide practical product decisions in mobile games.

Approach

I synthesized three established taxonomies into a single, industry-oriented framework. I designed the research protocol and recruited 30 participants who met defined screening criteria. The framework works in three steps:

Evidence

I analyzed 30+ semi-structured interviews through reflexive thematic analysis, generating 954 qualitative codes. I facilitated cross-team affinity mapping in Miro and resolved coding disagreements so the themes held up.

Worked example: Hole.io

The thesis applies the framework to three mobile games. In Hole.io, the way players were made to watch ads was one of the most cited reasons for quitting. Here is that exit point traced through all three steps.

Outcome

A practical framework, plus resource-conscious design recommendations that balance user trust, retention and monetization. The aim is to improve the player experience while keeping viable revenue models. The framework has not yet been tested inside a game studio; validating it in industry is the next step.

Methods

  • Semi-structured interviews
  • Screening and recruitment
  • Reflexive thematic analysis
  • Inductive coding
  • Affinity mapping
