Automated Trading Bot

A rules-based bot that trades the S&P 500 once a day without emotion, and stays safe while doing it.

Product
Paper-trading bot
Role
Designer and developer
Stack
Python, GitHub Actions, Alpaca

Background

Many people want their savings to grow but do not have time to watch the market every day. Emotion also gets in the way: people buy when prices are high and sell in a panic when prices drop.

Problem

Can a small set of clear, tested rules make steady trading decisions without emotion, and stay safe while doing it? The bot also has to be easy to stop and easy to understand.

Approach

I designed the bot around safety first and returns second. It trades only on a paper (simulated) account, and the code cannot switch to real money.

How it works

Every trading day after the close, the bot reads the closing prices and queues its orders, which fill when the market opens the next morning. The account runs two strategies side by side.

Dips (60%)

  • Buy SPY after a short dip, but only while the long-term trend is up (price above its 200-day average and 2-day RSI below 10).
  • Sell when the price recovers above its 5-day average, after 5 trading days, or at a 3% loss.

Trend (40%)

  • Hold an S&P 500 fund (IVV) while it stays above its 200-day average.
  • Sell when it closes below that average, and buy back when it closes above again.

Idle cash sits in a T-bill fund (BIL) so it earns interest between trades.

Past results do not predict future returns. This is a learning project on a simulated account, not financial advice.

Safety by design

  • No new buys after a 2% loss in a day or a 5% loss in a week.
  • A one-switch kill switch pauses the bot and cancels open orders.
  • API keys stay in GitHub secrets, never in the code.
  • Discord alerts for every trade, large move, loss limit and error. Quiet days send nothing, so alerts stay meaningful.

Testing

  • Backtested on 10 years of S&P 500 history, with 0.05% per side charged to account for slippage.
  • A comparison tool runs the strategies side by side with alternatives (other indexes, trend only, and plain buy and hold) and checks that results hold in both halves of the period.
  • Automated tests run on fake data, so no keys are needed.

Takeaways

Good automation design means honest limits. I documented the known gaps: the stop-loss is checked once a day, orders fill at the next open so an overnight gap can move the price, and paper fills skip slippage. Being clear about what a system cannot do is part of earning a user’s trust.
